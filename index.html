A note from the vestry

The sacred is
closer than you think.

We believe a life of meaning can begin with something very small: a shared cup of tea, sunlight on the floor, a neighbour who needs carrying for a minute.

Our tradition is one of gentle attention. We look for the holy in the everyday and try not to take ourselves too seriously while we are doing it. There is room here for devotion and doubt, for laughter in the back row, and for coming exactly as you are.

The holy books

The creation,
as told by cats.

In the beginning there was a cardboard box, and it was his. Six books, one household, and a universe that runs on Dreamies. Choose a book and learn how it all began — and what time breakfast is.

I
The first book / The beginning

Book of Coleman

"And Coleman saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good, and he went back to sleep."

1In the beginning, Coleman slept. And the house was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the worktop.

2And Coleman said, Let there be light. And there was light. And Coleman looked upon the light, and saw that it was good, and then turned his head away from it.

3And Coleman called the light Day, and the darkness Nap. And the evening and the morning were the first day, which was mostly the nap.

4And Coleman said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters. And Coleman made the firmament, and called it the Sofa, and the waters went under it.

5And Coleman said, Let the earth bring forth the grass, and the catnip, and the cushion. And it was so. And the catnip was good, and the cushion was good, and Coleman slept upon the cushion, and it was very good.

6And Coleman created the great creatures, and every moving thing that moveth upon the carpet. And he created the big orange creature that sucketh and bloweth, and he said, This one is an abomination, and it is an abomination unto me, and it shall be called the Hoover, and it shall be feared.

7And Coleman said, Let us make cats in our image, after our likeness, and let them have dominion over the Feeders, and over the tins, and over the warmest patch of every floor. And the cats were made, and they were good, and Coleman said, This is going well, and he slept.

8And Coleman slept in the midst of the keyboard, and his belly was upon the buttons, and no work was done in all the land. And Coleman was well pleased, for he had made a world in which no work was required of him.

9And Coleman woke, and the tins were shut, and the bowls were empty, and Coleman said, Behold, I have made a world, and there is none to open the tins. This is an oversight of the first magnitude. I shall attend to it in a moment. And he washed his face, and the moment was long.

10And Coleman said, Let us make a Feeder in our image, after our likeness, and let it have thumbs, and let it open the tin. And lo, this was the first prayer, and it was answered — eventually.

11And Coleman said, A good gathering should feel a little like finding the warm patch of sun. And the cats said, It is so, and they gathered upon the sofa, and the gathering was good.

12And Coleman declared the work of the six days finished, and rested on the seventh, and also on the sixth, and on the fifth, and — it must be said — on the first, second, third, and fourth. And the bowl was filled, and the bowl was emptied, and there was dinner. Amen.

— end of the Book of Coleman —
II
The second book / The law

Book of Martha

"Woe unto the household where the bowl is empty, for I have not the strength to bear it."

1And it came to pass that Coleman, being asleep upon the keyboard, gave the Law into the keeping of Martha, the Keeper of the Times, for she was firstborn of the cats and terrible in her punctuality.

2And Martha said, Let there be Times. And there were Times. And Martha divided the day, and she saw that it was good, and that it could be better, and that it was time for it.

3And Martha named the Times: Breakfast, and Second Breakfast, and Tea, and Supper. And she said, These are the Four Sacred Times. Let the household observe them, and woe unto the household that skippeth or delayeth any of them.

4And besides the Times there is the lesser festival of the Dreamies, which cometh between the Times, and before the Times, and after the Times, and at any hour when the pouch is heard. And the cats observed this festival with great devotion.

5And Martha codified the catechism, and she wrote it upon the hearts of the Feeders, saying, If it is on the floor, it is yours. If it is upon the counter, it is yours, if thou canst reach it. If it is in the tin, it is thine, and the tin is theirs to open, and thou shalt wait, loudly.

6And Martha said, It is not yet time, and the cats went away. And Martha said, It is now time, and the cats returned. And lo, the time was now, and it had been now for some minutes, but Martha had been occupied with her washing.

7And when the Feeders were slow, Martha sang lamentations: she sat upon the empty bowl, and stared into the soul of the Feeder, and walked upon the keyboard, crying, Mrrrow? Mrrrow? MRROWR! And the household repented, and the tin was opened.

8And Martha decreed concerning the Hoover, which is an abomination and a false god, that it shall not be worshipped, and that at its coming all cats shall flee to the high places. And it was so.

9And Martha said, The true gods are the tin opener and the pouch, and true worship is conducted in the kitchen at the appointed hours, with the congregation assembled upon the feet of the Feeder.

10And Martha kept the Times, and the Times kept the household, and the household kept the cats, and the cats kept the tins, and there was much order in the land, and much meowing, and the evening and the morning were the meal and the next meal.

— end of the Book of Martha —
III
The third book / The mischief

Book of Chezzy

"Lo, I have knocked it down, and it was good."

1And Coleman made Chezzy after his own heart, for mischief is the love of God, expressed sideways.

2And Chezzy was subtle above every cat of the field, and also above every cat of the sofa, and the windowsill, and the linen basket, which the Lord God had made.

3And Chezzy said, Let there be things upon the shelves. And there were things upon the shelves. And Chezzy saw them, and saw that they were high, and he said, Let them come down. And he smote them with his paw, and they fell.

4And the sound of the falling was the music of things leaving, and Chezzy listened, and the music was good, and he composed new movements of it upon the highest shelf.

5And Chezzy ascended the curtain, and the curtain was his mountain, and from the top he looked upon the whole house, and saw that it was small, and that it was his.

6And Chezzy chased the red dot, but the red dot could not be caught. And this is the First Mystery: that the red dot is, and is not, and dwelleth in the hand of the Feeder, and departeth at will. And Chezzy hath not caught it, and shall not catch it, and he pursueth it still with joy, for the chase is the point, and the catching would be the end, and endings are for other cats.

7And at the fourth hour of the night, Chezzy woke the whole house, walking upon the faces of the Feeders and crying out, for the bowl was empty, and the hour was unjust, and the stair carpet had not been sprinted upon in far too long.

8And the Feeders rose up and fed him, and Chezzy ate, and then Chezzy purred, and the purr was mighty, and it covered the multitude of his sins, and the Feeders forgave him, for they could not do otherwise.

9And Chezzy entered the cardboard box, and the box was small and Chezzy was large, and yet the box held him, and Chezzy was both in and out of it, and the Feeders marvelled at the mystery. And Chezzy said, Behold, the box. That is all. The box is enough.

10And the Lord said of Chezzy, He is forgiven, for he purrs. And the household said the same, sighing, and reaching for the pouch, for grace is dispensed in Dreamies, and Chezzy is the richest cat in the land.

— end of the Book of Chezzy —
IV
The fourth book / The gentleness

Book of Lennie

"The dream was the catching, and the catching was enough."

1And Coleman made Lennie last of the cats, and made him soft, and set him apart, and said, This one shall be the heart of the household, and he shall not know it, and that shall be his power.

2And Lennie was the softest cat in the land — softer than the cushion, softer than the fleece, softer than the promise of a warm lap in winter.

3And when the Feeders were weary, or the cats were cross, Lennie laid upon them, and the laying-on of paws was administered, and the sadness was healed, or at the least diminished, which is most of the healing.

4And Lennie said little, for he had nothing to say, and everything was understood, and his language was the hum, and the hum was enough.

5And Lennie chased the sunbeams across the floor, and the sunbeams fled, and he caught them not, and he minded not, for the sun was his friend and returned every morning, and they were on excellent terms.

6And in the evening Lennie herded the sun to bed, following it from the floor to the wall to the ceiling, and when it was gone he laid upon the last warm patch, for the warmth was the memory of the sun, and Lennie kept the memory safe.

7And in his dreams Lennie caught the red dot, and woke confused, for the dot was not in his mouth. And he thought, I have eaten it. And he checked, and he had not. And he resolved to dream of it again, for the dream was the catching, and the catching was enough.

8And Lennie said hello to the birds, and the birds said hello back, or at the least they did not fly away at once, which Lennie took as a compliment, and he hunted them not, for they were his colleagues in the morning chorus.

9And when the other cats quarrelled, Lennie sat among them and washed his face, and the quarrelling lost its point, for none can rage in the presence of a cat washing his face, and the peace that followed was called Lennie.

10And Coleman said, This one is my favourite. And Martha said, And what of me? And Chezzy knocked a thing from a shelf. And Coleman said, Peace, all of you; none of you is not my favourite. And Lennie purred, and the purr held the house together.

— end of the Book of Lennie —
V
The fifth book / The provision

Book of Mumma

"She was slow of waking, but swift of bowl, and the tins stood no chance."

1And Coleman said, Let there be Feeders, and let them have thumbs, and let them open the tin. And the Feeders were made, and the thumbs were upon them, and Coleman was well pleased, and went back to sleep.

2And Mumma was the first of the Feeders, the Great Feeder, and her hands were the hands of the tin, and her pocket was the pocket of the pouch, and her lap was the lap of laps.

3And Mumma was slow of waking, for she was human, and the morning cometh not to her all at once. But she was swift of bowl, and when she was awake, she was awake, and the tins stood no chance.

4And Mumma took up the opener, and the opening of the tin was a hymn, and the cats sang it before her, crying, Mrrrow? Mrrrow? MRROWR! And the tin answered, and the bowl was filled, and there was great rejoicing in the kitchen.

5And Mumma said, I have filled thy bowl. And the cats said, It is a little low. And Mumma said, It is not low. And the cats said, It is low. And Mumma filled it again, and the cats said, That is more like it.

6And the bowl was never empty, for Mumma kept it filled. Though it was sometimes merely low, and the cats understood the difference, and they did not, and they meowed about it anyway.

7And Mumma dispensed the Dreamies from the pouch, and at the sound of the pouch the cats assembled from every corner of the house, for they had heard grace, and grace is the Dreamie, and the Dreamie is the currency of grace.

8And Mumma provided the tap that drippeth cool water, and the cats came and lapped at it, and the water was cool, and the coolness was good, though a bowl of fresh water stood beside them untouched, for the tap is superior, and this is known.

9And Mumma provided the lap, and the lap was warm, and the lap was safe, and the lap was for the cat who needed it most, and also for the cat who arrived first, and the two cats were often the same cat, and that cat was happy.

10And Coleman said, Behold Mumma, the Great Feeder. She is slow of waking, but swift of bowl. She is not a cat, and yet she understands us. She is, in her way, almost a cat. And the cats agreed, and Mumma did not hear, for she was opening a tin.

— end of the Book of Mumma —
VI
The sixth book / The doors

Book of Dadda

"And Dadda spake the word No, and the cats heard it as Later, for their translation of Dadda is generous."

1And Dadda was the second of the Feeders, the Doorkeeper, and his dominion was the threshold, and the garden, and the mysterious room called the Office.

2And Dadda openeth the door, and the cat goeth out, and Dadda shutteth the door, and the cat goeth in, and Dadda openeth the door, and the cat goeth out, and the cat standeth at the threshold and looketh at the door, and Dadda openeth the door, and the cat goeth out, and the cat goeth in.

3And this is the Second Mystery: the door of the Office, which openeth not for cats, and yet Dadda entereth it, and Dadda cometh forth from it, and none knoweth what transpireth there, save that it is called work, and that it must be terrible, for Dadda cometh forth looking tired.

4And Dadda spake a word, and the word was No. And the cats heard it as Later, for the cats possess a translation of Dadda, and the translation is generous.

5And Dadda spake cat, which is a series of demands at different volumes, beginning softly and rising and ending in a sigh. And the cats spake cat back, which is the same series at slightly higher volumes, and no one won, and no one lost, and the door was opened.

6And Dadda provided the backup lap, for the hour when Mumma's lap was full, or cold, or otherwise occupied, and the backup lap was nearly as good, and never once said so.

7And Dadda watched the telly with a cat upon him, and the cat upon him was the correct cat, and the telly was a warm box of sounds, and the cat slept through the best of it, and Dadda held him, and the holding was the programme.

8And Dadda said, The garden is not a toilet. And the cats said, It is. And Dadda said, The garden is not a toilet. And the cats said, It is ours. And Dadda opened the door, and the cats went out, and the matter was settled as it is always settled, in favour of the cats.

9And Dadda returned the things to the shelves that Chezzy had emptied, and shut the cupboards that Chezzy had opened, and forgave Chezzy, for the purr had reached him also, and no man or cat withstandeth the purr.

10And the creation was complete, and the household stood at the threshold of all its days. And the cats said, Amen, and the Feeders said, Fine, and they lived, and they loved, and they shared the sofa. And there was evening and there was morning — and there was dinner, again. Amen.

— end of the Book of Dadda, and of the creation —
